My review

1/5Overall rating

My fifth Arkitec kit and the first I would warn people off. Poor fit, confusing instructions and a janky dragon, with the lighting the only feature that earns its keep.

My daughter bought this kit for me because she is a big Shrek fan and wanted the finished model. This was my fifth build from Arkitec. I had previously built three of the company's little television kits and one stadium, so I went into this with a reasonable idea of what to expect.

Unfortunately, this kit did not live up to those expectations.

Challenge 2/5

This is not a difficult kit on the surface. There are 290 pieces, no complicated gear system, and nothing that should make it especially demanding.

It did present challenges, but they were not the enjoyable kind that test your building skills. Most of the difficulty came from poor fit, awkward assembly, unclear instructions, and parts getting in the way of other parts.

Enjoyment 2/5

Very disappointing.

I really enjoyed the three little television kits I built from Arkitec. While I found the finished stadium underwhelming, it was still a nice little build.

This kit seemed to be in my way from start to finish. Instead of settling into the build and enjoying the process, I spent most of my time trying to figure out what the kit wanted me to do and how I could make it cooperate.

Engineering and Fit 1/5

This simply did not go together well. Pieces were difficult to get into position, and there were several places where I cut tabs off and glued the parts into place instead.

The kit also has several nearly impossible fits because previously installed parts block access to pieces that need to be added later. That problem could have been reduced with a better assembly sequence, but the instructions did not provide one.

The wiring layout was also substandard and made an already awkward build more difficult than it needed to be.

I spent a lot of time fussing with the flying dragon, and I finally concluded that it is just a bad design. The movement is very janky and never looks smooth or convincing.

Materials and Finish 3/5

The kit is mostly MDF with some acrylic pieces. The acrylic was a nice touch and added some variety to the materials.

Unfortunately, the MDF sometimes felt soft and spongy. That caused breaks that probably would not have happened with better material, and the tabs deformed easily when pieces had to be removed and reinstalled.

That became a particular problem because the confusing assembly order forced me to take several sections apart and rebuild them.

Instructions 1/5

The instructions were very confusing. In several places, the order in which parts were installed was extremely important, but that order was not made clear.

I repeatedly discovered that something already installed was blocking the next piece. I then had to take things apart, change the order, and reassemble everything. The soft MDF was not on my side during that process.

Better instructions would not have solved every problem with this kit, but they could have prevented a lot of unnecessary frustration and damage.

Display Appeal 3/5

The finished model is not especially impressive. It looks rough around the edges and does not have the polished appearance I expected from the box.

The flying dragon is janky, noisy, and distracting. The rotating Fiona figures do not add much for me either. The lighting is the one feature that adds some real value to the display, but this kit desperately needed music or sound effects to cover the noisy, herky-jerky dragon mechanism.

It is recognizable as Shrek, and a serious fan may enjoy it for that reason, but the famous characters cannot completely overcome the rough execution.

Value 1/5

I found this listed online for around $27. At that price, you can get a much better kit, a better building experience, and a more polished finished display.

I feel bad that my daughter paid that much for it. She wanted a Shrek model, and I wanted to build something special for her. Now I feel like I need to find a better kit to make up for this one.

Overall Rating 1/5

A rating this low feels harsh for a company whose kits I have enjoyed before, but this does not compare well to Arkitec's little television models. Those cost around $12, go together nicely, have good lighting, and make attractive displays. This kit costs considerably more and delivers considerably less.

It also feels like it falls into an old licensed-product trap. A company gets the rights to use a famous property, puts familiar characters on the box, and relies on fans buying it without giving enough attention to the quality of the product inside.

I have fallen into that trap with other brands often enough that I now cast a wary eye on almost anything carrying famous branding. Sometimes the license adds something worthwhile. Other times, as with this kit, it feels like the name on the box is expected to do most of the work.

This was supposed to be a fun Shrek build for my daughter. Instead, it became one of the most disappointing kits I have completed.
