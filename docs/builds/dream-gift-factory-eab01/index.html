Challenge 5/5

There were a lot of gears and a lot of little pieces. The build bordered on frustration and occasionally crossed over.

Enjoyment 4/5

The frustration kept this from receiving a perfect enjoyment score, but it was still a very rewarding project.

Engineering and Fit 4/5

The gearwork was spot on. As usual, I waxed every piece of wood that moved against another piece. Some of the building fit was challenging. I also spent a lot of time trying to correct a wobbling Christmas tree before discovering that the topper above the star helped stabilize it.

Materials and Finish 3/5

The kit included a couple of extremely thin boards. I am careful and experienced, but I still had several breaks, including two while sanding. Perhaps my sanding style is too aggressive.

There were plenty of extra pieces, so I never needed glue to repair a broken part because a replacement was available. I also encountered some splintering that added to my painting work. Several pieces were colored on only one side even though the reverse remained visible, while some colored surfaces were buried inside the model, which I found curious.

Instructions 5/5

No complaints. They were detailed and easy to follow.

Display Appeal 6/5

This is an exceptional display model, right up there with the Coffee Factory, and it should become a favorite with my grandchildren.

Value 5/5

It was not an overly expensive kit and should provide years of display value, even if it is packed away and brought out only for the holidays. I also appreciate the USB power instead of batteries, which makes it far more practical.

Customization

I did a lot of painting, changed colors in several places, added holiday figurines, put a teddy bear in the chimney, and added a dog, of course. I did not like most of the pieces left as plain wood, so I stained them and was very pleased with the result.

Overall Rating 5/5

This earns a five based on display appeal alone. It is a complex mechanical Christmas showpiece and one I expect the grandchildren to remember.
