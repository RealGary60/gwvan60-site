**Challenge: 3/5**

For a complex project, it went together fairly easily. I had some problems with the seagulls, but those were caused by my failure to read the instructions carefully.

**Enjoyment: 4/5**

A few minor obstacles reduced the score, but overall it was a fun build.

**Engineering and Fit: 4/5**

The gearwork was flawless, and the mechanism runs smoothly and very quietly. As usual, I waxed every piece of wood that moved against another piece. The shaft in the tower does not have all the support I think it needs and wobbles slightly, but overall this is a well-designed kit.

**Materials and Finish: 3/5**

I am not a fan of the paper-covered boards. The paper absorbed ink from my edge markers and tended to loosen during heavy handling. It is fine if everything goes together correctly the first time, but the paper on the tabs does not hold up well to repeated assembly.

I also colored the backs of many pieces. I often do that anyway, but there were some especially obvious unfinished surfaces here. I would not have been happy leaving bare wood on the backs of the seagulls.

**Instructions: 3/5**

The instructions were adequate, but they were not in booklet form and did not include a preview of the pieces required for each step.

**Display Appeal: 5/5**

This is a very attractive mechanical model that looks great beside the Coffee Factory and Dream Gift Factory.

**Value: 4/5**

It costs less than the Coffee Factory or Dream Gift Factory, although it also has less going on mechanically.

**Overall Rating: 4/5**

If you are intimidated by the Coffee Factory or Dream Gift Factory, and they can be daunting, I would recommend this as an intermediate step. It is closer in complexity to Happyduke Toys. In the end, you get a distinctive mechanical display piece that you will be proud to show.
