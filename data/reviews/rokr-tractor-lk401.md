This is a very different ROKR model from the company's newer kits. It is large, built from heavy wood, and powered by a spring-driven mechanism. The design reminded me more of something from one of the Eastern European model manufacturers.

This tractor definitely made me work for it. I needed my rubber mallet several times, and installing the spring nearly defeated me. Nearly. I powered through, got everything working, and enjoyed the challenge, even when I was not sure whether I was building the tractor or fighting it.

The kit did not include any wax, although the moving parts almost certainly needed it. Fortunately, I had my own and applied it anywhere friction might interfere with the mechanism.

I bought John Deere green and yellow paint specifically for this build. I wanted it to be a tribute to my farm-boy heritage and to the tractors I first learned to drive more than 55 years ago. The classic colors really brought the model to life.

Despite the difficulties, I am very happy with the finished tractor. It is substantial, mechanical, and unlike anything in ROKR's current lineup. I continue to scan eBay for these older ROKR kits because there is just something about them that appeals to me.

After wrestling with this one, however, I think it is time to move on to something newer, lighter, and a little more cooperative.
