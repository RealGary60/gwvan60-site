I won this kit in a contest on Craftime, and from the beginning my plan was to customize it for the winter contest there. I had accumulated a lot of holiday accessories, both from my Craftime kit and from other sets I picked up for holiday and gift builds, so I already knew the finished Polaris Post was not going to look much like the one on the box.

This is a three-star kit with 434 pieces. It is not especially difficult, but there is certainly plenty to do.

**Challenge: 3/5**

The box says three stars, and three stars feels right. There are no mechanical elements and the lighting is simple. It is not a tough build by any stretch of the imagination, but the large number of pieces keeps you busy.

**Enjoyment: 4/5**

This was a fun, easygoing build, although it included a couple of things that are not really for me. I tend to avoid book nooks containing dozens of tiny books because I find assembling them repetitive. Give me gears, not repetition.

There is also a lot of paper and quite a few stickers, neither of which is my favorite part of building. I know plenty of other builders would consider all those little accessories heaven, so that is entirely a matter of personal taste. The kit also uses a lot of double-sided tape, which I tossed aside in favor of my trusty B7000 glue.

**Engineering and Fit: 5/5**

The fit was excellent. The house frame was not fiddly at all and went together solidly without needing any reinforcing glue. There is no complicated engineering involved, but I always appreciate a kit that does not make me fight the fit. The tabs on this one snapped neatly into place.

**Materials and Finish: 5/5**

There are a lot of boards in this kit, with a good variety of colors and wood stains. The plastic pieces were well made and blended nicely with the wood. Their placement also reduced the amount of exposed-edge work, so I did very little edge coloring on this build.

Not a single piece broke and needed repair, which is a rarity for my clumsy hands. The little construction details and accessories were especially well done.

**Instructions: 4/5**

The instructions were good, although I found the order confusing at times and ended up doing quite a bit in reverse. I prefer to assemble the primary structure first so I can see where everything will eventually go. This kit often has you build the accessories before the surrounding structure.

The instructions were in booklet form and included materials lists before the steps, so they earned bonus points from me for that.

**Display Appeal: 5/5**

This is a great-looking Christmas decoration even before customization. Mine is already out and will fit perfectly with our holiday decorations. I am also looking forward to adding the Reindeer Express when I build it.

**Value: 5/5**

I won the kit in a contest, so my personal value was obviously excellent. At the prices I found online, however, it is not an expensive kit, especially with all the holiday sales going on.

Because so many of the accessories can be built separately and at the same time, this would also make a great family holiday project. Several people could work on different pieces and then bring everything together for the final assembly.

**Customization**

I had so much to add that I realized very early that a minor customization was not going to be enough. Polaris Post needed a serious expansion.

I had saved the wooden frame that my Wooden City Roadster came in because I could not bear to throw it away, and its time had finally come. Unfortunately, this completely validated my habit of keeping things that "might come in handy later."

I reinforced the corners, painted the frame red, and added LED lighting underneath for an underglow effect. I then rounded the corners on a small piece of plywood and painted it white to create the new base.

Some leftover pieces from my recent Robotime helicopter build became part of the terrain. I added Titebond and the snow flocking from my Craftime kit, and then it was simply a matter of deciding where everything should go.

As the display grew, so did the story. Polaris Post has become overwhelmed because the elves have been slacking off. Packages and letters are piling up, the list of deliveries is overflowing, and Santa has been forced to leave his desk and make an emergency delivery run while Donner tries to get the post office back under control.

Did I have fun with this? Absolutely. What started as a few minor upgrades snowballed, quite literally, into a much larger project.

**Overall Rating: 5/5**

Overall, this was a very positive experience. The kit went together well, gave me a terrific foundation for customization, and became a Christmas display that I am very happy to have in the house.

I would recommend Polaris Post to anyone looking for an approachable holiday build, especially someone who enjoys creating lots of little accessories. I am already looking forward to adding the Reindeer Express and expanding the scene even further.
