I finished the ROKR Happyduke Toys kit for a charity auction in November. My hope was that someone would see it as more than a model and give it a place in their Christmas display.

I kept the Christmas customization fairly restrained. I added a few snowflakes to represent the first snowfall of the year and the beginning of the Christmas season at the toy store. I also added an elf and snowman to the balcony, along with a bear holding balloons by the front door that I particularly liked.

The front display window received most of my attention. I added a toy soldier I already had, a gingerbread boy from a Rolife Creative Kit, and my favorite detail, a tiny boxed Rolife carousel honoring the first wooden model I ever built.

There are several little teddy bears tucked into the windows, and I replaced the original cat coming out of the chimney with another bear because I always replace the cats. I also added a dog because no build of mine is complete without one.

Naturally, I colored the exposed edges and painted the backs of several pieces that otherwise would have remained unfinished.

This was not the most challenging ROKR model I have built, but it was a good and enjoyable project. A few loose pieces needed glue, and I managed to snap one roof piece into three sections while trying to horse it into place. Fortunately, it repaired well enough, and the toy store survived my construction methods.

I was very happy with the finished result. I hoped it would bring a decent price at the auction, raise some money for charity, and become part of someone's Christmas display for years to come.
