Santa is supposed to come out of the little door every hour, ride all the way around, and go back in. On my build he did it sometimes. Other times he stopped partway, or never appeared at all. If that is happening to you, here is everything I tried, and the one thing that actually fixed it.

**Start with the batteries, even if the clock still keeps time**

This is the answer on my build, so I am putting it first.

My clock kept perfect time and the hands moved normally, so batteries were the last thing I suspected. But the hands need very little power. Driving Santa out of the door and all the way around takes much more. A set of batteries can have plenty left for timekeeping and not quite enough for the trip.

My clock had been on the wall for several weeks when the original batteries finally died. I put fresh ones in, and Santa started rocketing all the way around, every hour, without fail. My theory is that I ran those first batteries down while I was fussing with the mechanism during the build, testing it over and over, and by the time the clock went on the wall there was not enough left in them.

So before you take anything apart: put in a fresh set. If Santa suddenly behaves, you are done.

**Check that Santa's track is clear and square**

If fresh batteries do not do it, look at the path he travels. Any rub along the way costs you power you do not have to spare.

Watch a full cycle with the back off and see exactly where he slows or stops. Look for the door binding against its frame, for the track sitting slightly out of square, and for a little fuzz of plywood left on an edge where a part was punched out. A fingernail or a light sand takes care of that last one.

**Look for glue where glue should not be**

I only needed glue in a few places on this kit, for reinforcement. If any of it crept into a moving joint, that joint will drag.

Check every pivot Santa's arm passes through. A joint that should swing freely by hand but does not is the one to look at.

**Do not over-tighten**

It is tempting to snug everything up, but the mechanism wants a little play. If you tightened things during assembly and Santa got worse, back them off slightly and try again.

**Test on the wall, not on the bench**

The clock behaves differently lying flat than it does hanging. Gravity is working with the mechanism in one position and against it in the other. Do your final test with the clock hanging where it will live.

**If he still sticks**

Run the mechanism through a few cycles by hand, slowly, and feel for the exact point where it catches. That spot is your problem, and it is almost always a rub, a glue spot, or a part seated a hair off.

I got mine working and I got to know this mechanism very well in the process. If you build this one and get stuck, I am there for you. Come find me on Reddit.
